About Cheshire

Settled in 1694, incorporated in 1780 — a New Haven County town that has kept its village feel through three centuries of change.

A brief history

Cheshire's story starts in 1694, when the area was first settled as part of Wallingford and known as New Cheshire Parish. It took nearly a century for the community to gain its independence: in May 1780, Cheshire separated from Wallingford and was incorporated as its own town.

The town takes its name from Cheshire, England — a connection many residents still feel in the town's green spaces and village feel.

How the town is governed

Cheshire operates under a council-manager form of government. A nine-member Town Council sets policy and direction, and a professional Town Manager carries it out day to day. The current Town Manager is Sean M. Kimball.

This structure is designed to keep decisions accountable to residents while handing the daily operations of the town to a professional administrator.

Landmarks

First Congregational Church

Cheshire's First Congregational Church is one of the town's best-known landmarks — a visible link to the community's long history.
